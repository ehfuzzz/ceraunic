## Judgement

Re-checked against the page reads. **The answer did not change. The reasoning about ForecastEx and Polymarket US did.**

**The question.** Does any venue price a 7-day single-city temperature cover at under 2x burn in the tails, and list it a week or more ahead? No. This is firmer than before the page reads. Every exchange ladder is now read as listing one to two days ahead (ForecastEx next day, Polymarket US today and tomorrow, Polymarket international about two days, Kalshi 37-39 hours). No exchange lists a weekly temperature contract. No over-the-counter seller publishes a price.

**Which venue could hedge the tail under 2x burn.**

- None is shown to, and one candidate is now ruled out. Before the page reads ForecastEx looked like the venue most likely to beat Kalshi, because one-degree strikes replicate a capped call exactly. The pages say it lists only about nine strikes, four each side of the forecast, the day before. A cover's tail is by definition far from the forecast, so the strikes that would hedge it are not listed. Where the forecast is already near the strike, the strip covers at most the first few degrees and never the cap, each contract costs one cent per side (a 25% load on a 4-cent strike), New York settles at LaGuardia, and the source is Weather Underground. ForecastEx is a shoulder hedge for Chicago and Miami on the day before, at best.
- Kalshi and Polymarket US both have open-ended end brackets, so the tail can at least be bought there. Kalshi's tails are measured at 4-7x burn. Polymarket US has the same bracket form and almost the same fee (0.0695 against 0.07), so there is no reason to expect its tails to be cheaper; its depth is unknown. What it adds is basis: the NWS climate report at Central Park, Midway and Miami Intl is exactly what the covers pay on, where Kalshi now reads The Weather Company.
- Over-the-counter carriers can price a tail layer on the exact station. By the usual pricing rule (U17, unverified) a layer that pays often prices near 1.2-1.6x burn and a remote tail at 2-5x, so "under 2x" is realistic only for an aggregate cover that attaches fairly low on a diversified book, and only above the carrier's minimum (about $50K premium at Descartes, confirmed; $100K premium for the one Arbol product that publishes one, confirmed; Munich Re works at $1M and up on the page that gives a number).

**Which solve timing but not basis.**

- CME: lists about three months ahead, but on a month's mean-temperature degree days at LaGuardia and O'Hare (stations now confirmed in chapter 403), with no Miami, no rain, no screen quotes, and a 20-lot block minimum ($400 per degree day). It hedges "was the month warm", not "did Tuesday reach 93".
- Monthly contracts on ForecastEx and Kalshi (US national monthly temperature, monthly rain totals): list ahead, wrong index.
- Over-the-counter carriers: solve timing and basis together, fail on size.

**Which solve basis but not timing.** Polymarket US (best match: right source, all three stations), Kalshi (right stations, The Weather Company source), ForecastEx daily (Midway and Miami Intl only, Weather Underground, narrow strike window), Polymarket international (closed to the US, two wrong stations). ### (a) How to read a CME degree-day quote with a broker

1. **No account needed: read the exchange mark.** Open the settlement page for the contract, pick the trade date, read the "Settle" column for the month. New York HDD: https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/weather/hdd/new-york-hdd-monthly/settlements. New York CDD: https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/weather/cdd/new-york-cdd-monthly/settlements. Chicago: swap `new-york` for `chicago` in either URL.
2. **What the number is.** A settle of 469.0 on New York HDD November is the market's (or, with no trades, the exchange's) expected sum over November of max(0, 65 - daily mean temperature) at LaGuardia. Each index point is worth $20. Long one contract at 469 makes $20 for every degree day the final index comes in above 469. One contract is therefore about 469 x $20 = $9,380 of notional.
3. **Check whether the number is a trade or a mark.** On the same page read "Prior day OI" and "Est. volume" for that month. Zero in both means the settle is an exchange mark and nobody has traded it. On 2026-10-01 that was true for October and November New York HDD.
4. **Compare with burn.** Take the 10-year and 30-year average of the same index for the same station and month from NOAA daily data (LaGuardia, mean of max and min, base 65 F). Settle divided by that average tells you whether the mark is rich or cheap to climatology. Inside the forecast window the mark moves with Speedwell's forecast, so compare only months that have not started.
5. **In the broker.** In a futures account search the product by its exchange code: H4 (New York HDD), K4 (New York CDD), H2 (Chicago HDD), K2 (Chicago CDD), then the month letter and year digit (V = October, X = November, Z = December, so New York HDD November 2026 would be H4X6) [unverified: the exact symbol format differs by broker]. In IBKR's Trader Workstation, use the contract search, choose Futures, exchange CME, and type the code. If nothing comes back, the broker has not enabled the product; ask their desk whether they clear CME weather blocks. Expect an empty book even if the symbol loads.
6. **Options are the instrument that looks like a cover, and they are not on a screen.** A call on the CDD index with strike K pays $20 x max(0, index - K). To get a price, the account's broker asks one of CME's three listed weather brokers (BGC Financial, Snapper Creek Energy, TP ICAP) for an indicative two-way market, and a trade clears as a block of at least 20 contracts, which is $400 per degree day. Asking for that indication is contacting a broker, so it is the owner's call and nothing was sent.
7. **Costs to add.** Exchange fee $1.35 per contract per side for non-members (2024 filing, re-read 2026-10-02; the 2026 schedule could not be read), the broker's commission, and margin.
